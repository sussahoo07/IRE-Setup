# PRE Shared Services Connectivity Addendum

## 1. Purpose and relationship to the PRE design

This addendum extends [`pre-foundational-services-connectivity-design.md`](pre-foundational-services-connectivity-design.md) with a service-by-service connectivity model for the shared services identified for cyber recovery. It also refines the existing design principle: the PRE must remain usable when Production and ordinary corporate identity/control planes are unavailable, so connections to external shared services are optional, narrowly scoped capabilities—not assumed dependencies.

This is a target design, not confirmation that these services are deployed or reachable. Service editions, hosting locations, tenant/account ownership, private connectivity options, vendor FQDNs/IP ranges, protocols, ports, identity flows, data handling, and licensing must be confirmed with each service owner and vendor before implementation. Do not copy generic port lists into firewall rules. Maintain a version-controlled service-flow register containing the approved, environment-specific values.

### Security posture

- **PRE-local first:** PRE-local AD/DNS, break-glass AWS access, recovery artifacts, logging, and baseline management must continue to work if every external service below is unreachable.
- **No Production trust:** do not connect PRE to Production AD, Production authentication bridges, Production Vault namespaces, Production CyberArk, Production DNS, or Production network-management systems if they may be compromised. Use dedicated PRE tenants, accounts, instances, namespaces, trust relationships, credentials, and policies where the service is required.
- **Outbound by default:** initiate approved connections from PRE through the controlled egress/inspection tier. Do not expose PRE services or management interfaces to the internet or corporate networks. Vendor-initiated inbound connections are prohibited unless separately threat-modeled and approved; prefer outbound polling/webhooks through a broker or relay.
- **Per-service activation:** each connection has a named PRE source, service destination, direction, protocol, data scope, owner, approver, activation window, expiry, logs, health check, and shutdown procedure. No transitive routing between SaaS, on-premises, Azure, and PRE.
- **Fail closed:** if a required service cannot be independently trusted or safely reached, suspend that integration and use the documented PRE-local/offline alternative. Do not weaken isolation to restore convenience integrations.

## 2. Connectivity patterns

Select one of these patterns for each service, and record the choice in the service-flow register:

1. **PRE-local instance:** a separately provisioned service instance, domain, tenant, or namespace in the PRE account or a dedicated recovery account. It has no trust or replication dependency on Production. Prefer this for essential identity, DNS, secrets, artifact, and management functions.
2. **Private service path:** a dedicated, non-transitive private path to a vetted service in a separate trusted account/VPC or cloud. Use a PRE-specific TGW route table, private endpoint/service attachment where supported, and inspection. Private routing does not by itself establish trust; retain application authentication and authorization.
3. **Controlled SaaS egress:** PRE initiates TLS connections through a dedicated egress proxy/firewall to vendor-published service endpoints. Enforce FQDN-aware policy where supported, validate certificates, restrict DNS resolution, log the destination and identity, and review data export. SaaS endpoints and IP ranges can change; automate vendor-approved updates with review and drift alerts rather than broad `0.0.0.0/0` rules.
4. **Artifact/API exchange:** exchange signed, scanned, encrypted artifacts or scoped API requests through a broker/store instead of providing broad network access. Apply checksums/signature validation, malware scanning, least-privilege tokens, expiry, and audit.
5. **No connection:** keep the path absent where the service is not required for recovery, its trust status is unknown, or a safe route cannot be established.

For hosted SaaS, use the vendor's current endpoint and network allow-list documentation for the exact tenant, region, product features, and agent version. Do not assume a stable single IP, a private endpoint, or that TCP 443 alone describes the complete dependency. Configure proxy bypass only when vendor documentation requires it and Security approves it.

## 4. Recovery-stage activation and approval

| Stage | Shared-service connectivity posture | Required actions and gates |
|---|---|---|
| **Dormant readiness** | No Production routes/trust. PRE-local foundations are maintained. Optional SaaS paths remain disabled except separately approved health checks that do not expose PRE workloads/data. | Validate PRE-local account, identity, DNS, PKI, artifact, management, and logging recovery. Review vendor endpoints and contracts. Keep signed recovery artifacts and offline runbooks current. |
| **Incident declaration and containment** | Disable/verify absent all Production, IRE, and unapproved enterprise routes. Do not activate external identity or automation automatically. | Incident commander declares the recovery environment. Network/security operators inspect TGW associations/propagations, firewall policies, DNS rules, credentials, and identity trust. Preserve evidence and validate deny paths. |
| **Foundation bootstrap** | Enable PRE-local services only. | Use independent break-glass; start PRE-local directory/DNS/PKI/Vault/management/logging as needed. Prove local authentication, name resolution, secret retrieval, time, and audit delivery before workload launch. |
| **Recovery build and hardening** | Use PRE-local code/artifact caches. Optional read-only access to one approved code/artifact service may be activated if required. SaaS security-agent callbacks may be enabled individually. | Approve each flow separately. Validate repository provenance, token scope/expiry, vendor endpoint list, telemetry/data handling, egress inspection, agent heartbeat, and local fallback. No broad access to Jira, source control, or cloud consoles is granted to recovered workloads. |
| **Validation** | Workload-to-PRE services enabled per workload dependency map. External service paths remain off unless a test explicitly needs them. | Require a documented test plan, source/destination allow-list, security review, monitoring owner, time limit, and rollback. Record test evidence and unexpected flow attempts. |
| **Business restoration** | Stage connections to rebuilt/declared-clean enterprise, Azure, or on-premises targets; Production remains blocked until separately approved as safe. | Use a clean target, newly issued credentials/certificates, restricted routes, firewall inspection, application-level authentication, phased traffic, and rollback conditions. Do not restore old trust relationships by default. |
| **Teardown and reconstitution** | Remove temporary paths and credentials; retain only approved steady-state PRE-local services. | Revoke API tokens, PATs, app registrations, certificates, grants, temporary routes, resolver rules, and firewall exceptions. Verify removal independently, review logs, rotate potentially exposed secrets, and update the service-flow register. |

### Per-connection approval record

Before enabling an external service connection, the request must capture:

- incident/change ID and business purpose;
- service name, tenant/region, service owner, and trust assessment;
- PRE source subnet, workload/security group, identity, and destination FQDN/IP/service endpoint;
- protocol, port, direction, DNS resolver, route, proxy, firewall policy, and any return path;
- data sent/received, classification, minimization/redaction, and retention;
- authentication method, credential scope, owner, storage, expiry, and rotation/revocation process;
- required approvals from the incident commander, PRE security owner, and network/service owner (plus data owner where applicable);
- activation start/end time, automated expiry or named teardown operator, monitoring/alert owner;
- positive and negative connectivity test cases, success criteria, and rollback/disable steps.

High-risk identity, privileged-access, routing, and bulk data-export paths require dual approval and a second-person verification after teardown. Apply changes through reviewed IaC or an auditable change workflow; record the before/after route, firewall, resolver, and identity configuration.

## 5. Firewall and service-flow register requirements

A firewall rule must be tied to a specific service-flow-register row. At minimum, the register should include:

| Field | Required information |
|---|---|
| Service / use case | Exact named integration and recovery activity |
| Owner / trust zone | PRE technical owner, remote service owner, and service hosting zone |
| Initiator / destination | PRE subnet, security group, workload identity, tenant, FQDN or endpoint; no broad remote CIDR unless justified |
| Protocol / port | Vendor-validated values for the deployed version and feature; include direction and stateful return behavior |
| Route / inspection | PRE route table, TGW attachment (if any), proxy/firewall path, DNS/resolver policy, and no-transit assertion |
| Authentication / data | Credential type and scope, data elements, classification, TLS/authentication properties, secrets storage, and rotation |
| State / expiry | Disabled, enabled, or scheduled; incident/change ID; start and expiry; auto-expiry or teardown owner |
| Telemetry / tests | Firewall, DNS, endpoint, and application logs; health check; permitted-path and denied-path test evidence |
| Fallback / rollback | PRE-local alternative, failure behavior, and precise disable/revoke actions |

Resolve SaaS destinations using the service's official, current documentation for the deployed product and region. Where FQDN filtering is unavailable, use vendor-published IP ranges with an owner and scheduled review; do not widen access to all internet destinations to accommodate endpoint churn. If the product requires dynamic endpoints that cannot be safely allow-listed, use a dedicated egress proxy, service-specific private connection where officially supported, or do not activate the integration.

## 6. Identity, secrets, and software-supply-chain safeguards

- Create recovery-specific tenants/projects/repositories, identity-provider applications, machine identities, API tokens, and service accounts. Do not reuse Production tokens, runners, signing keys, secrets, or vault policies.
- Prefer workload identity/federation and short-lived credentials over long-lived PATs or static API keys. Where static secrets are unavoidable, scope them to read-only/minimum actions, store them in PRE-controlled secret storage, set expiry, monitor use, and revoke them after the recovery window.
- Keep human interactive access separate from machine-to-machine access. Require MFA and just-in-time privilege for responders; use service identities for automation with pinned audiences, restricted claims, and explicit repository/workspace/environment scope.
- Treat code, modules, container images, packages, pipeline definitions, and policy bundles as untrusted until provenance and integrity are checked. Pin versions/digests, verify signatures/checksums, scan, and retain the exact approved artifact used for a recovery deployment.
- Restrict outbound uploads. Prevent source code, secrets, memory dumps, customer data, and forensic evidence from being sent to SaaS tools unless the data owner and incident commander approve the exact data flow. Redact telemetry and traces at the PRE-local collector.
- External logging/monitoring is supplemental. Preserve local CloudTrail, Flow Logs, host logs, security events, and recovery records in a protected PRE-controlled location so a SaaS outage or account lockout does not erase the incident record.

## 7. Acceptance tests

Before approving this design for production recovery, exercise the following in a representative PRE environment:

1. **Isolation baseline:** with all optional routes disabled, prove PRE cannot connect to Production, IRE, on-premises, Azure, external SaaS, or arbitrary internet destinations. Confirm denied attempts are logged.
2. **Local foundations:** disconnect optional shared-service paths and demonstrate PRE-local administrator authentication, workload DNS, required domain authentication, certificate issuance/validation, secrets retrieval, and audit logging.
3. **External service enablement:** for each approved service, enable only the registered source/destination/protocol; prove the intended transaction works and unrelated PRE workloads and destinations remain denied.
4. **Credential containment:** verify no Production identity, runner, API token, Vault, CyberArk safe, certificate/private key, or trust relationship is required or accepted by PRE.
5. **Failure behavior:** simulate SaaS/provider, identity bridge, DNS, egress firewall/controller, and monitoring outage. Confirm PRE remains locally manageable, logs locally, and blocks rather than bypasses unavailable controls.
6. **Supply-chain controls:** fetch a pinned approved artifact, validate provenance/signature/checksum, and reject an unapproved version or invalid signature without launching it.
7. **Lifecycle teardown:** expire a temporary Jira/source-control/repository/security-vendor/enterprise connection; verify routes, firewall and resolver rules, tokens, certificates/grants, and sessions are removed or revoked and the removal is logged.
8. **Restoration boundary:** prove a PRE connection to a clean restoration target is separately approved and does not create transitive access to Production or other attached networks.

Record the test date, build/version, configuration revision, operators, approvals, results, log references, exceptions, and remediation owner. Repeat after material route, identity, vendor endpoint, or foundational-service changes and as part of the organization-approved recovery exercise cadence.

## 8. Decisions required from service owners

For each listed service, the service owner must provide: hosting model and region; recovery tenant/instance availability; official current endpoint and protocol documentation; supported private connectivity and proxy model; authentication and credential lifecycle; data transmitted and retention; outage behavior; licensing/capacity; recovery owner and RTO; and an approved offline/local alternative. Until these are documented and tested, the service remains **disabled by default** and is not a PRE recovery prerequisite.
